How to calculate zakat on gold

Zakat on gold is calculated in a few steps: confirm whether your gold reaches the nisab, convert its weight to the unit your price uses, apply the 2.5% rate, and record the result for the year. This guide walks through each step.

Jump to the steps

Step 1: Know which gold is zakatable

Opinions differ on some gold, such as gold worn as jewellery for personal use. Decide, or ask a scholar you trust, how your school of thought treats each item before you add it. The calculation only works as well as the list of assets you include.

Step 2: Choose your nisab standard

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that makes zakat due. Gold and silver are both used as standards, and they give different thresholds. Common references are 85 grams of gold and 595 grams of silver. Check which standard applies to you before you calculate.

Step 3: Convert the weight

Gold prices are often quoted per gram, per troy ounce or per tola, depending on the market. Make sure the weight and the price use the same unit. If your gold is measured in tola, convert it carefully, because a small conversion error changes the result.

Step 4: Value the gold at today's price

Multiply your weight by the current price per gram. Use the price on the day you calculate, from a source you trust, and note the date and source in your records.

Step 5: Check the nisab

Compare your total zakatable wealth, after deducting debts you owe, with the nisab for the standard you chose. If your wealth is below the nisab, no zakat is due this year. If it reaches the nisab, continue to the next step.

Step 6: Apply the 2.5% rate

Zakat on wealth that has reached nisab and been held for a full lunar year (hawl) is 2.5% of the total zakatable amount. Some assets and rulings differ, so confirm the details with a scholar when your situation is unusual.

Example

Illustration only: suppose you hold 100 grams of gold, the price is a placeholder of 100 units per gram, and your other zakatable wealth and debts net to zero. Your gold is worth 10,000 units. If that exceeds the nisab for your standard, zakat is 2.5% of 10,000, which is 250 units. Use the current prices and your own figures for a real calculation.

Step 7: Record the year

Save the calculation, the nisab used, the price and the amount due. Then record each payment as you make it, including partial payments, so you can see what remains for the year.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay zakat on gold jewellery?

This depends on your school of thought and on how the jewellery is used. Many people ask a scholar they trust about their own gold before deciding.

Which nisab should I use?

Use the standard your school of thought follows, or the one a scholar you trust recommends. Zakat Book lets you choose between gold and silver.

Does Zakat Book give religious rulings?

No. Zakat Book calculates the amount from the figures you enter and the standard you choose. It leaves classification decisions to you.
