How to track real estate commissions and deals

Commission income arrives in pieces, often months after a deal starts. A simple record for each closing, with the split, fees and expenses written down as you go, makes every payout easy to check and every tax season easier to handle.

Jump to the steps

Step 1: Create a record for every deal

Start a record when a deal goes under contract. Note the property address, the client, the sale price, the key dates and the expected closing date. One record per deal keeps each transaction separate, even when several are closing in the same month.

Step 2: Calculate the commission

Work out the gross commission from the sale price and the commission rate in your agreement. Then apply the split with your brokerage, and deduct any fees the brokerage or your agreement charges. Write each figure down, so you can see how the final number was reached.

Step 3: Track the payout dates

Record when you expect the commission to be paid, and when it actually arrives. If a payment is late or short, the record shows the gap, so you can raise it with the title company or brokerage while it is still easy to resolve.

Step 4: Log expenses against each deal

Keep receipts for costs you pay for a deal, such as marketing, mileage, photography and software. Linking an expense to the deal it belongs to makes it simple to show what each transaction really earned.

Step 5: Review your pipeline each month

Look at deals under contract, deals closing this month, and commission still owed. This gives you a realistic view of income, and shows which deals need follow-up.

Step 6: Keep records ready for tax time

Store deal records, commission statements and expense receipts in one place, and keep them backed up. Ask an accountant which records they need, and check the rules that apply in your country and state.

Commission tracking checklist

  • A record created for every deal under contract
  • Gross commission, split and fees written down
  • Expected and actual payout dates recorded
  • Expenses logged against the deal they belong to
  • Monthly pipeline and commission-owed review
  • Records stored and backed up for tax time
Check your brokerage and tax rules. Commission splits, fees and deductible expenses depend on your brokerage agreement, your state or country, and your own situation. Confirm the details with your broker or accountant before you rely on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my commission on a sale?

Multiply the sale price by the commission rate in your listing or buyer agreement. Then apply your brokerage split and deduct any fees. Use a commission calculator to check the numbers, and keep the inputs with each deal.

Should I track expenses for each deal?

Yes, where you can. Linking expenses to a deal shows its true profit and makes tax records easier to assemble.
