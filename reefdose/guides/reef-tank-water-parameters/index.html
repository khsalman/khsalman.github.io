How to track reef tank water parameters

Stable water is the foundation of a healthy reef. Testing consistently, logging the results and watching the trends tells you more than any single reading. This guide covers which parameters to track, how to read them over time and how to dose with a calculator instead of guessing.

Jump to the steps

Step 1: Choose the parameters to track

Most reef keepers track the same core group:

  • Alkalinity (dKH), which buffers pH and is used heavily by stony corals.
  • Calcium, a building block for coral skeletons.
  • Magnesium, which helps keep calcium and alkalinity stable.
  • Salinity, which should stay steady to avoid stressing livestock.
  • pH, temperature and nutrients, such as nitrate and phosphate, depending on your setup.

Track any custom parameter your test kits measure. Consistency matters more than the number of parameters.

Step 2: Test on a schedule

Test at the same time of day, with the same kit, and record each result right away. Changes are easier to spot when the readings are comparable. Many keepers test alkalinity, calcium and magnesium weekly, and salinity more often if they top off with fresh water.

Step 3: Read the trends, not just the readings

A single reading can be affected by sampling error or a recent water change. Look at the trend over days and weeks. A slow drift in alkalinity or calcium usually tells you more than one high or low result.

Compare against your own target. Pick a target range for your tank and your livestock, and check it against your test kit's instructions and a trusted reef-keeping source. Targets differ between SPS-heavy tanks, mixed reefs and fish-only setups.

Step 4: Dose with a calculator

Dosing supplements to the right amount for your tank volume keeps you from overshooting. A dosing calculator uses your tank volume, the current reading, your target and the product's strength to estimate how much to add.

  • Change one thing at a time. Adjust one parameter, then wait and test before changing another.
  • Dose in small steps. Spreading a correction over several days is safer than one large change.
  • Track stock levels. Know when your bottle will run out so you can reorder before your dosing stops.

Step 5: Keep up with maintenance

Water changes, equipment servicing and parameter tests are easy to forget. Set reminders for each one, and log the work next to your readings so you can see whether a maintenance routine changes your numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I test reef tank water?

Test the parameters you dose regularly, such as alkalinity, calcium and magnesium, on a weekly schedule. Check salinity more often if you top off. Adjust the schedule to your tank and what you see in the trends.
