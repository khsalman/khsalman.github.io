How to make a home inventory for insurance

If your home is damaged or items are stolen, an up-to-date inventory makes a claim much easier to document. This guide shows how to record each room and item, keep the proof, and store the record somewhere you can reach it after an emergency.

Jump to the steps

Step 1: Go room by room

Start at the front door and work through each room, including the garage, storage areas and outdoor spaces. Recording by room means nothing is missed, and it helps you picture the layout if you ever need to explain a loss.

Step 2: List each item

For each valuable or expensive item, record:

  • What it is, with the brand and model if you know them
  • Where it is in the home
  • Approximate purchase date and price
  • Serial number, where there is one
  • Its condition, and any damage you can see at the time

Step 3: Take photos and keep proof

A photo of each item, plus any visible labels or serial numbers, is more useful than a written description alone. Keep receipts, invoices and warranty documents with the item they belong to. Scan paper proof while you have it, so it does not get lost.

Step 4: Record the big items and systems

Appliances, heating and cooling systems, and major furnishings often have the most value and the most paperwork. Note the installation or service date and any warranty expiry date, since these affect repair and replacement decisions.

Step 5: Store the record safely

Keep a copy somewhere other than the home itself, such as an encrypted cloud backup or a trusted person's records. A list stored only on the device that might be lost or damaged is less useful. Update the inventory after major purchases or renovations.

Step 6: Review it every year

Set a yearly reminder to check the inventory against your current belongings. Add anything new, remove items you no longer own, and check that the values still reflect what you would need to replace them.

Home inventory checklist

  • Every room and outdoor space listed
  • Valuable items recorded with a photo
  • Purchase dates, prices and serial numbers noted
  • Receipts, invoices and warranties attached to the items
  • Major appliances and systems listed with service and warranty dates
  • Copy stored outside the home
  • Yearly review reminder set
Check with your insurer. Insurers differ in what they ask for and how they value items. Ask your insurer or agent what documentation they accept before you rely on a specific format.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need receipts for everything?

Receipts help most for valuable items. If you do not have one, a photo, a bank or card statement, or a warranty record can still help. Record what you know and note where the information came from.
