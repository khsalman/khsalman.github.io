How to track insurance policy renewals as an agent

Missed renewals cost clients and commission. A renewal tracking routine, with one record per policy and reminders set well ahead of each expiration date, keeps your book of business under control. This guide shows how to build that routine.

Jump to the steps

Step 1: Record every policy in one place

List each policy with the client name, carrier, policy number, product type, premium and expiration date. A single record per policy means you can see the whole book at once, instead of searching across emails and spreadsheets.

Step 2: Set reminder lead times

Decide how far ahead you need to act on each renewal, such as 60, 30 and 14 days before expiration. Longer lead times give you room to review coverage, gather updated information and talk to the client before a lapse can happen.

Step 3: Review before the renewal date

When a reminder fires, check whether the client's needs have changed. Look at new property, vehicles, dependents, or income changes, and note anything that should be discussed before the renewal is processed.

Step 4: Track the outcome of each renewal

Record whether a policy renewed, was replaced, was cancelled, or lapsed, and why. Over time this shows which renewals need earlier attention and which clients are at risk of leaving.

Step 5: Forecast commissions from the renewal book

Use your expiration dates and premiums to estimate upcoming commission for each month. A forecast lets you plan follow-up work around the months with the most renewals, instead of reacting to them when they arrive.

Step 6: Keep client records private and backed up

Client and policy details are sensitive. Store them on a device you control, protect it with a passcode, and make sure you have a backup you can restore. Follow the privacy rules that apply in your state and to your carriers.

Renewal tracking checklist

  • Every active policy recorded with its expiration date
  • Reminder lead times set for each renewal
  • Client needs reviewed before each renewal date
  • Outcome recorded for every renewal, including lapses
  • Monthly commission forecast reviewed
  • Records backed up and protected
Check your carrier and state rules. Renewal notice requirements, commission rules and client privacy rules vary by carrier and state. Confirm what applies to you before you rely on a process.

Frequently asked questions

How early should I start a renewal?

Many agents start 30 to 60 days before expiration, which leaves time for a review and any changes the client wants. Use the lead times your carriers and state require as the minimum.

What if a client does not respond before the renewal date?

Record each attempt in the policy record, follow your agency's procedure, and note what the client was told. A clear record protects both you and the client.
