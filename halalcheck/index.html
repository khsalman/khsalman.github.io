How it works

Three ways to check any product

Scan a barcode, photograph a label, or search an ingredient manually. HalalCheck gives you a clear verdict in seconds.

📦

Barcode Scanner

Point your camera at any product barcode. HalalCheck fetches the ingredient list from OpenFoodFacts, analyses every ingredient offline, and returns a verdict in under two seconds.

📸

Label OCR Scanner

No barcode? No problem. Photograph the ingredient panel directly. On-device OCR extracts the text, the engine finds the ingredient block automatically, and you get a full breakdown.

🔎

Ingredient Search

Search any ingredient or E-code by name. See the verdict, the reason, and every alias the engine recognises -- in English, Arabic, French, German, Turkish, Indonesian, and Urdu.

🗄️

178+ Ingredient Database

A curated offline database of 178+ named ingredients with multilingual aliases, covering common additives, emulsifiers, enzymes, gelatins, and animal-derived substances.

🔢

446+ E-Code Database

Full coverage of E100 to E1521. Every E-code is individually classified with a verdict and a plain-language explanation of its source and halal status.

📋

Scan History

Every scan is saved locally. Browse your full scan history, filter by verdict, and re-check any product without scanning again. Your data stays on your device.

Clear, honest verdicts

Three outcomes. No guessing.

HalalCheck never invents a halal verdict for an unknown ingredient. If it cannot confirm, it says so.

✅

Halal

Every identified ingredient is confirmed halal. The product is safe to consume based on its listed ingredients. A percentage breakdown shows the confidence split.

⚠️

Doubtful

One or more ingredients could not be confirmed -- either because the ingredient is inherently ambiguous, or because it was not recognised by the database. Exercise caution.

🚫

Haram

At least one ingredient is confirmed haram (e.g. pork gelatin, alcohol, lard). The offending ingredients are highlighted with their reason clearly stated.

HalalCheck verdicts are algorithmic estimates based on ingredient names. They are not a fatwa or religious ruling. Always exercise personal judgement and consult a qualified scholar for your specific needs.
